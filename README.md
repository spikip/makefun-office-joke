# One-Line Office Joke: 24-Hour Sprint

A one-line office joke, written by a small Python program to `/output/entry.txt`:

> Our standup is just nine people taking turns reading their calendars out loud to a manager who bills it as 'alignment.'

## Run

From the repository root:

```
python3 main.py
```

This writes the line (119 characters, UTF-8) to `/output/entry.txt`
(set `OUTPUT_DIR` to write somewhere else).

## About this entry

The setup is a ritual every office worker knows: the daily standup,
meant to take fifteen minutes and somehow eating the whole morning.
Nine people stand in a circle and, one by one, read their own
calendars out loud — which is what most standups quietly become.
Nothing is decided and nothing is unblocked; it is a roll call with
better branding. The turn arrives with the manager, who watches all
of this and does not fix the meeting but renames it instead.
Reading calendars out loud becomes "alignment," a word strategic
enough to bill a client for. That final word carries the joke: the
gap between what the meeting is and what it is called is exactly
the gap everyone in the room has felt but never said out loud.
